Emergency Burst Pipe Water Damage Cleanup in Orem, UT

A burst pipe in an Orem home can send a lot of clean water into walls and ceilings fast, and the first two days decide what survives.

What burst pipe cleanup means

If a pipe has split and water is still running, treat it as an emergency. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and while you wait you can close the shutoff, move rugs and electronics out of the room, and put buckets under any dripping ceiling.

Burst pipe water damage cleanup in Orem, UT is the removal of water and the drying of the building after a pressurized supply line, fitting or fixture connection fails suddenly. This page covers that sudden failure. Pipes that split from freezing are covered on the frozen pipe restoration page, and bulk pumping of standing water is described under emergency water removal.

How burst pipes happen in Orem homes

Orem incorporated in 1919 largely to build a water system, according to the Utah State History Encyclopedia, and the city still runs that system through the Water Section of Public Works. Your house sits downstream of the city meter, so a split in the main line before the meter is a call to the city, while everything after it is yours. Find the meter and main shutoff today.

Most local bursts are ordinary wear. Braided supply hoses age, compression fittings loosen, and copper or plastic lines crack at joints. Burst pipes are common in Orem, mostly in winter and in older kitchens and bathrooms, and the quiet ones inside a wall are the costly ones.

When a burst pipe is an emergency

A supply line holds water under pressure, so a break keeps pushing water until someone closes a valve. A split under a sink can fill a cabinet in minutes. A break in an upstairs wall can soak insulation, drip through a ceiling and reach a finished basement before anyone notices.

That is why calls at night and on weekends matter here. The dispatcher sends licensed, insured crews and offers same-day response when available, including after hours. Tell the dispatcher which floor the pipe is on, whether the water is off, and which rooms below are wet, so the crew can bring the right extraction units and air movers.

First-hour safety steps

  • Close the fixture stop valve first, then the main if water keeps coming.
  • Cut power to wet rooms only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • For sparks, a gas smell or water at outlets, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us.
  • Open a faucet at the lowest level to drain the line once the main is off.
  • Stay out from under a sagging ceiling, because a wet ceiling can drop without warning.
  • Photograph the break, the wet rooms and any damaged belongings before you move them.

How the crew cleans up a burst pipe

  1. Find the source and the water path

    The crew confirms the shutoff is holding, then follows the water from the break down through floors and walls. A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show wet drywall and insulation that look dry from the room.

  2. Extract the free water

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull water off hard floors and out of carpet. Pooled water in a cabinet or a basement corner comes out first, because it feeds everything else.

  3. Open what is holding water

    Wet insulation, soaked ceiling drywall and damaged baseboard are cut out when they cannot dry in place. Small access holes let air reach wall cavities that can be saved.

  4. Set up drying equipment

    Air movers push air across surfaces, and LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. The layout follows the moisture map, not a fixed number of machines per room.

  5. Clean and treat

    Surfaces get cleaned, and antimicrobial treatment goes on where water sat. Contents are sorted into what can be dried and what should be discarded.

  6. Monitor until the readings match dry

    Daily moisture checks compare wet areas against a dry reference reading. Equipment comes out only when the materials reach the dry goal, following the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard as the published reference.

The first 48 hours before clean water turns gray

A burst supply line starts as clean, Category 1 water. It does not stay that way. Once it soaks carpet, pad, old drywall and dust, and sits for a day or two, it picks up bacteria and starts to smell. The industry calls that slide toward Category 2, and it changes what can be saved.

Fast extraction and airflow keep more material in the salvage column. Delay moves carpet pad, ceiling insulation and the lower edge of drywall into the discard pile. If you cannot reach anyone for hours, lift furniture legs onto foil, run any ceiling fans, and keep the house cool enough that you are not baking the damp.

Fixture shutoff, main shutoff and the plumber

A fixture stop valve under a sink or behind a toilet shuts one line and leaves the rest of the house running. The main shutoff stops everything. If the stop valve is stuck or the break is in a wall, go to the main and turn it fully closed.

Our crews dry and clean the building, and a licensed plumber repairs the pipe. Orem's Building Safety Division says plumbing work generally needs a permit, so ask the plumber who is pulling it. Ask the city before you open walls for the rebuild, and keep the old section of pipe for your insurer to see.

What shapes the cost of burst pipe cleanup

How long the water ran

A break caught in minutes wets one room. A break running overnight reaches ceilings, framing and the floor below.

How many floors and rooms got wet

Water that traveled through a ceiling into a finished basement means more rooms to extract and dry.

What the water touched

Hardwood, carpet over pad and insulated ceiling cavities each need different handling.

Equipment and drying time

More air movers and dehumidifiers running for more days adds to the job.

Demolition and contents

Cut-out drywall, discarded pad and packed-out belongings are separate pieces of the scope.

Sudden and accidental water damage, like a pipe that splits, is often covered by a homeowners policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Mistakes to avoid after a pipe bursts

Do not wait to see whether the ceiling dries by itself. Do not run a household vacuum on a wet floor, and do not stand under a sagging ceiling. Painting over a brown stain seals moisture in and hides the problem.

Do not toss everything in a pile. Set aside damaged items for your adjuster and photograph them first. The same steps apply in nearby towns, and you can see how we handle calls in Provo and Lindon. For the drying phase itself, see water mitigation.
