Aspen at a glance

Aspen is a neighborhood in Orem, UT. This page uses what Orem's own neighborhood plan says about Aspen housing and turns it into first steps for a leak, a burst pipe or a wet floor.

Aspen shares its plan with Timpview, and the blocks differ a lot, so the right first move depends on whether you own, rent or manage the place. For the wider picture, see Orem water damage restoration and the Orem service area page.

What those numbers mean when a pipe fails

Block group C is 91.1% owner-occupied, so a wet floor there is usually the homeowner's call. In block group B the plan lists 58.8% renters and multifamily as the majority type, so a leak can involve a tenant, a landlord and the unit below.

Renters should tell the landlord or property manager in writing right away and ask where the building shutoff is. Owners should find their main shutoff valve before a leak happens.

The plan says most Aspen and Timpview housing was built from 1960 to 1980, with newer single-family homes north of 1600 North. Build eras use different materials, so a restoration crew reads each house on site. The plan also counts two mobile home parks near 1500 North and State Street, about 70 homes in all.

Between State Street and Geneva Road

The plan places Aspen and Timpview between State Street and Geneva Road, with 800 North, 1200 North, 1600 North and 2000 North as east-west corridors. Interstate 15 separates the east side from the Geneva Road side. Landmarks include Bonneville Park, Aspen Elementary and Bonneville Elementary.

The plan maps detention basins here, and Orem has about 20 citywide, according to Fox13. It does not mention basements, flooding or drainage problems, or how many homes have basements, so check your own house. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address, because a zone applies to mapped areas and not to a whole neighborhood.

Where indoor water problems usually start

Supply lines and fittings

A failed toilet connector or faucet line can soak cabinets and subfloor before anyone notices.

Appliances

Washer hoses and ice-maker lines are common. See appliance leak water damage cleanup.

Burst pipes

A split pipe moves water fast. Burst pipe water damage cleanup starts with stopping the flow.

Unit to unit

An upstairs leak can stain a ceiling below. Both households should photograph the damage.

What a restoration visit involves

  1. Find where the water went

    A restoration crew checks the source and maps wet walls and floors with a moisture meter.

  2. Extract standing water

    Extraction comes first in water damage cleanup.

  3. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers pull out moisture, which is the aim of water mitigation.

  4. Monitor until dry

    Readings repeat until materials are dry. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard.

Water safety steps before anything else

Keep people and pets out of contaminated or electrically hazardous water. Shut the water off only if the valve is safe and reachable. Do not use electrical equipment or touch breakers while standing in water.

Call 911 or the utility for sparks, a gas smell or sewage at outlets. Photograph the damage when safe, and lift unaffected items only when safe.

Sewage and storm water are Category 3. Do not dry them yourself or use shop vacs or household fans.

Insurance and documentation for owners and renters

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms, so ask your insurer. The Herald Extra reported in 2023 that most homeowners insurance does not cover flooding, and flood insurance takes 30 days to take effect.

Renters should check their own policy and tell the landlord. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help document the loss.

What to tell the person who answers the phone

  • Where the water came from, if known
  • What is wet, and whether a unit below is affected
  • Whether the water is shut off
