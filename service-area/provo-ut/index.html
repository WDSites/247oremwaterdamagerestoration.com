When water is spreading in Provo

Water is moving through a Provo kitchen, basement or apartment hallway, and drywall, carpet pad and framing are soaking it up while you read. Call the dispatch line as soon as the room is safe to stand in, because crews work from a dispatch order and every wasted minute gives the water more material to reach. The restoration professionals we dispatch are licensed and insured.

Provo has a local problem that most towns do not. Much of the city's storm system was converted from old flood irrigation canals and pipes, so a street drain that backs up may be feeding a line with a long, patched history. If water rises through a floor drain or a yard stays flooded after rain, report it to Provo City Public Works too, since the city owns the pipes in the street.

If water is still spreading in Provo

1

Stop the source

Turn the fixture shutoff clockwise. A bigger leak means closing the main valve where the line enters the building. Renting? Tell the manager at once, because the main in an apartment building is often shared.

2

Power down wet areas only if safe

Check the floor around the electrical panel first. Dry floor and an easy reach mean you can switch off breakers for the wet rooms. Water near the panel, outlets or sewage means stay out and call 911 or the utility before calling us.

3

Lift what you can carry

Laptops, paperwork, shoes and keepsakes go up onto counters and beds. Clean-water rugs can come off the floor. Leave heavy furniture where it sits, since dragging it across wet flooring tears the surface.

4

Keep everyone out of contaminated water

Water that came from a drain, toilet or storm line should be treated as contaminated. Children and pets belong in a dry room, and nobody wades through it barefoot.

5

Call the 24/7 line

Describe the water source, the number of wet rooms and anything unsafe. The dispatcher lines up a crew and explains what to do next. Photograph the damage with dates showing while you wait.

Why old canal pipes matter in Provo storms

Provo City Public Works runs a Storm Water section that began in 1992 as the Provo City Storm Water Special Service District. Because much of that system was converted from flood irrigation canals and pipes, capacity and condition differ from block to block. Ask Public Works what drains your street and whether any known backup problems exist near your address.

Provo also keeps a Flood Information page on floodplains. Pull up the floodplain map for your exact address before you buy, rent or finish a basement, and note whether the Provo River Parkway trail corridor or the lake side of town is anywhere near you. A lot near the river deserves a sump pump check each spring.

Student housing and rentals near BYU

Brigham Young University enrolls more than 30,000 students, and a large share of Provo's apartments and basement units are rentals. When water hits a rental, the paper trail matters as much as the pump. Report the leak to the landlord in writing, photograph every wet surface with dates visible, and keep the message thread.

Tenants should also ask whether renters insurance covers their belongings, since the building policy usually covers only the structure. Landlords should keep a record of the shutoff location for each unit so a roommate or a neighbor can close it quickly the next time something fails.

Foothill lots below Rock Canyon and Y Mountain

East Provo homes sit below Rock Canyon and Y Mountain, which means slope and runoff. After a hard storm or a quick snowmelt, check that gutters are clear, downspouts discharge away from the foundation and window wells are not holding water. A well that stays full against a basement wall is one of the easiest problems to fix before it becomes a flooded room.

What changes the scope of a Provo job

Water category

Clean supply water costs less effort than storm or drain water, which needs removal of porous materials and sanitizing.

Area and depth

More rooms, more floors and wicking up walls mean more equipment and more days of drying.

Materials involved

Hardwood, subfloor, plaster and insulation dry differently from carpet and drywall, and some must be replaced.

Rental or owner-occupied

Multi-unit buildings add coordination with managers and neighbors, and sometimes shared plumbing.

Every job is different, so call and describe what you see to get your real number. On coverage, sudden and accidental water damage is often covered while gradual leaks are often not, and a standard homeowners policy does not cover flood, so ask your insurer before you assume either way.
