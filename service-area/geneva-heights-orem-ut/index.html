The first subdivision in Orem

Geneva Heights is a neighborhood in Orem, UT with an unusual origin. According to the city's history page, it was the first subdivision built in Orem. Private developers put it up during World War II to house defense workers and their families for the Geneva Steel plant.

This page is for Geneva Heights residents with water in the building now or damage found later. It covers the housing, what to do first, and what to say when you call about Orem water damage restoration.

Finding Geneva Heights on the map

Geneva Park, at 260 N 700 W, is the easiest landmark. The city history page lists Aspen Elementary, Orem Junior High and Timpanogos High as schools near it and the plan also names Geneva Elementary and Orem Elementary. If you are unsure your address belongs here, the Orem neighborhoods page lists the others.

Where water comes from in a mixed block

Appliance and supply-line leaks

A washing machine hose, dishwasher line or refrigerator supply line can wet a floor slowly. See how appliance leak cleanup works.

Wet floors between units

In a duplex or fourplex, a leak upstairs stains the ceiling below. Both households and the manager should know.

Drain backups

A backed-up drain can bring dirty water into a lower unit. See sewage backup cleanup.

How a restoration job usually runs

  1. Find the source

    A crew finds the cause, such as a failed supply line or appliance, and the water is stopped or isolated.

  2. Extract standing water

    Pumps and extractors remove standing water, and soaked pad or materials may be pulled out.

  3. Dry with air movers and dehumidifiers

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers pull moisture from floors and walls. This is the core of water mitigation.

  4. Monitor until dry

    Repeated moisture readings continue until materials reach the dry standard in ANSI/IICRC S500, a published reference standard.

Water safety before anything else

Keep people and pets out of any water that may be contaminated or near electricity. Shut the water off at the fixture or main only if you can reach it safely.

Do not touch breakers or use electrical equipment while standing in water. Call 911 or the utility for sparks, a gas smell or sewage at outlets. Photograph the damage when safe, and lift unaffected items only when safe.

Sewage and storm water are Category 3. Do not try to dry them yourself, and do not use an ordinary shop vacuum or household fans.

Insurance, landlords and documentation

Coverage depends on the cause of the water and your policy terms, so ask your insurer. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope of work help document the loss.

Most homeowners insurance does not cover flooding, and flood insurance takes 30 days to take effect, according to a 2023 Herald Extra article.

Renters should tell the landlord in writing with photos. See water damage cleanup for what to record.

What to tell the person on the phone

  • Your address and unit number
  • Whether you own, rent or manage the building
  • Where the water came from, if you know
  • Whether the water looks clean or dirty
