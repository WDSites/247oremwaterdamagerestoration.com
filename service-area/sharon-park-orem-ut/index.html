Sharon Park and its housing

Sharon Park is a neighborhood in Orem, UT that the city plans together with Orem North. The combined area covers 704 acres. When a pipe fails or a water heater gives out here, the damage usually starts inside the house and spreads into walls, floors and ceilings.

This page explains what the city plan says about the housing, what to do in the first minutes, and what to tell the person who answers when you call about Orem water damage restoration.

Edges and landmarks

The city plan names State Street, 800 N (Canyon Parkway) and Center Street as edge roads. The plan text gives no street-by-street boundary, so use these as a rough frame and not as an exact line.

Sharon Park itself sits next to Sharon Elementary and has a pavilion, a playground and a walking path. City Center Park is nearby, with six baseball diamonds, pavilions and an Arboretum. Residents asked for lighting and safety along the North Union Canal, which the plan proposes as a trail.

Housing facts from the plan

Built before the 1980s

The plan says the neighborhoods mainly consist of houses built before the 1980s. Build eras use different materials, so a crew reads each house on site.

Low density, single-family

Low-density areas are almost exclusively single-family. A leak in one house usually stays in that house, so the record you keep is your own.

Small multifamily pockets

Medium-density areas have twin homes, quadplexes and small apartments at roughly 6 to 7 units per acre. Village Park Apartments is the only high-density development. Ceilings can leak into the unit below.

2010 housing count

The plan reports 3,251 units, with 3,075 occupied and 176 vacant, or 5.41 percent. Ask neighbors to report drips in empty units.

Flood maps and drainage

The plan does not mention basements or flooding, so check your own house for a basement or sump. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address, because a zone applies to mapped areas and not to a whole neighborhood.

For standing water in a room, read about water removal before the dry-out starts.

Safety first when water is inside

Keep people and pets away from contaminated water and from anything electrical. Turn off the water at the fixture or main only if you can reach the valve safely.

Do not use electrical equipment or touch breakers while standing in water. Call 911 or the utility for sparks, a gas smell or sewage at outlets. Photograph when safe, and lift unaffected items only when safe.

Sewage or storm water is Category 3. Do not dry it yourself, and keep ordinary shop vacuums and household fans away from it.

Documentation and insurance

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms, so ask your insurer what applies. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help document the loss.

Flood coverage is usually separate from standard homeowners policies. The Herald Extra reported in 2023 that most homeowners insurance does not cover flooding, and that flood insurance takes 30 days to take effect.

If a ceiling leaks from the unit above, tell the property manager in writing the same day.
