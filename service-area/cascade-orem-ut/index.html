Cascade at the mouth of Provo Canyon

Cascade is a neighborhood in northeast Orem, UT, at the mouth of Provo Canyon. This page is a practical first-response guide for a wet house, plus a plain look at what the public record says about the area.

The city's Canyon View, Orchard and Cascade plan says Cascade and Orchard were largely built first, with some of the first northeast subdivisions starting in the early 1970s. It does not report how often homes here take on water. For the wider picture, see our Orem water damage restoration page.

Where Cascade sits

According to Orem's plan, Cascade sits in northeast Orem where the city meets the mouth of Provo Canyon. The plan text gives no exact edges, so go by your own address.

Cascade Park and Cascade Elementary are in the neighborhood. The Murdock Canal Trail has a trailhead at 800 North and 1100 East, and the plan says UDOT is securing funding for sidewalk and stormwater improvements on 800 North. The North Union Canal runs through Cascade and Orchard.

Housing and drainage facts, and what they mean for you

The plan says about 93% of housing across the three neighborhoods is single-family detached, with accessory apartments, attached homes and duplexes making up the rest. In a detached house, the owner usually handles the whole problem.

The plan mentions legal accessory apartments, often basement apartments, but not how many homes here have basements. Check your own house. If someone rents space in yours, say so on the phone.

The plan does not address flooding or drainage problems. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address, because a zone applies to mapped areas and not to a whole neighborhood.

The August 2024 storm in northeast Orem

On August 13, 2024, a severe rain and hail storm hit Orem. Public Works Director Chris Tschirki told Fox13 the city got about an inch of rain in roughly 30 minutes, more than a 100-year storm, and said city drainage is designed for 10 or 25 year events.

The Daily Herald reported northeast Orem was the most affected area, judged by tree and limb damage, and some homes built below road grade took 2 to 3 feet of water. The articles do not name Cascade for any flooding, so do not read them as saying Cascade flooded.

Where storm and runoff water tends to enter a house

Window wells

A well that fills with rain can push water over the sill. Look for staining and wet carpet below the window.

Grade and downspouts

Ground that slopes toward a foundation sends water to the house instead of away.

Doors and walkouts

Water can pool at a threshold and track under flooring. Check baseboards.

Plumbing and appliances

Not every wet floor is storm water. Say what you saw.

What a restoration crew does after water gets in

  1. Find the source and category

    Clean supply water is Category 1. Storm water and ground water are treated as Category 3 until shown otherwise.

  2. Extract standing water

    Pumps and extractors remove what is on the floor first.

  3. Map the moisture

    Meters check drywall, subfloor and framing and show how far water traveled.

  4. Dry and monitor

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run while readings are rechecked. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard.

Safety first in a wet Cascade house

Keep people and pets out of water that may be contaminated or electrically live. Storm water and drain water are Category 3, so do not dry them yourself and do not use ordinary shop vacs or household fans.

Shut off water only if the valve is dry and reachable. Do not touch breakers or use electrical equipment while standing in water. Call 911 or the utility for sparks, a gas smell, or sewage at outlets. Photograph damage when safe, and lift unaffected items only when safe.

Documentation and insurance questions

Coverage depends on the cause and your policy terms, so ask your insurer. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help document the loss.

A 2023 Herald Extra article reported that most homeowners insurance does not cover flooding and flood insurance takes 30 days to take effect. Confirm with your insurer which applies to you.

What to tell the person on the phone

  • Where the water came from, if known
  • Which rooms are wet and whether any are below ground
  • Whether a window well, door or yard grade was involved
  • Whether anyone rents a basement apartment
  • Any sewage smell or water near outlets
