When water is spreading in Lindon

Water on a Lindon floor at two in the morning is still an emergency, and our line answers at that hour. The first useful thing you can tell the dispatcher is which pipe it came from, because Lindon homes run two separate water systems and the answer changes how the crew handles the cleanup.

One system is culinary, meaning drinking water. The other is the pressurized irrigation system, called secondary water, and it is not drinking water. A broken sprinkler riser and a split kitchen supply line both flood a room, but they start in different places and are shut off at different valves.

Late April to mid-October: the secondary water season

Lindon's secondary system runs from about late April to mid-October and is billed year round. In those months a cracked sprinkler line, a stuck zone valve or an open hose bib can pour water against a foundation all night. If your yard turns soggy on a dry day, shut the secondary valve and check the basement window wells.

Cross-connections between the secondary and culinary systems are prohibited, so a plumber should never join the two. If you suspect water from the irrigation side has entered a house line, say so on the call and keep the area closed off. Crews classify water by its source, and secondary water is not clean.

Late fall to spring: drain, shut and watch the thermometer

Lindon asks residents to drain sprinkler lines and shut their secondary valves before winter. The reason is practical, since a line left full can split in a freeze and then flood a yard or a crawl space when the system restarts in spring. Mark a calendar date each autumn for the drain and the valve shutoff.

Nights below freezing from late fall into spring also split exterior hose bibs and exposed pipes in garages and crawl spaces. Disconnect hoses, open the outdoor spigot and leave cabinet doors open on cold nights along exterior walls. A split pipe often shows itself only when the thaw begins and water appears under the sink.

If water is still spreading in Lindon

1

Where is the shutoff?

A fixture valve closes the leak at a sink or toilet. For a split line, shut the home's main valve instead. If the water came from irrigation, close the secondary valve at the stop-and-waste valve or the secondary meter.

2

Is the panel dry?

Only touch the breakers when the floor around the panel is dry and you can reach it without crossing water. Then switch off the wet rooms. If water is near outlets, the panel or sewage, stay away, and phone 911 or the utility first.

3

What can go up?

Shelves, counters and beds hold the small things: chargers, files, shoes, photo albums. Rugs on clean water can come up. Large furniture stays put.

4

Who needs to leave the room?

Anyone small or four-legged moves to dry ground. Water from a sewer line or a drain is contaminated, and irrigation water is not for drinking or splashing in.

5

Ready to phone?

Dial the 24/7 dispatch line with the source, the rooms involved and any hazards. Take dated photos while you wait for the crew.

Which side of the meter is the leak on?

Lindon City maintains the culinary water line up to and including the meter. The owner maintains everything past it. A leak on the street side of the meter is a call to Lindon City Public Works, while a leak in the yard line, under the slab or inside the house belongs to you and your plumber.

The secondary line works similarly. The city is responsible up to the stop-and-waste valve or the secondary meter, and the homeowner takes over beyond that. Before you pay for a dig, locate the meter and the valve and decide which side the water is rising from. That one decision usually settles who fixes the pipe.

Springs, wells and the Dry Canyon drainage

Lindon's culinary water comes from springs east of the city and from wells. Dry Canyon drainage northeast of Orem and Lindon flows to a detention basin near the Dry Canyon trailhead. In a wet spring, point downspouts and sump pump discharge well away from the house so you are not adding to the load on that drainage.

String Town and the country lots that remain

Lindon was once called String Town because houses lined the road between Orem and Pleasant Grove, and the town motto still reads a little bit of country. Part of that heritage is older buildings, from the Lindon Cider Mill built in 1857 to the Alfred Harper House of 1876, the only Lindon property on the National Register. When your home or outbuilding is old, tell the dispatcher its age, because older pipe and plaster change the drying plan.

Lindon incorporated on March 24, 1924, and is home to Gillman Farm, a Utah Century Farm, and the city Veteran's Memorial. Larger lots mean longer irrigation runs and long hose lines, so walk the whole property when you shut the water off.
