Sunset Heights is a large and mixed part of Orem

Sunset Heights, Orem, UT is a big neighborhood, and the city plan treats it as two halves, East and West. The plan estimates 4,050 housing units, which is a lot of different buildings with a lot of different water problems.

A leaking dishwasher in a farm-lot house and a failed water heater in an upstairs apartment need different handling. This page covers both, starting with who is responsible and what to document. For the wider picture, see our Orem water damage restoration page.

Where the plan places it

Orem's Sunset Heights East and West plan names Geneva Road, University Parkway and State Street as framing corridors. It mentions the western edge of the city near Utah Lake and treats UVU as the adjacent university anchor.

Sleepy Ridge Golf Course influenced development here, and Orem Elementary is mentioned in the plan. The plan gives no street-by-street boundary in its text, so go by your own address. For other parts of town, see the Orem service area page.

Housing facts and what they mean when water gets loose

The plan says the neighborhood is predominantly owner-occupied at 63%, with 37% renters, and 95% of units occupied. That mix means many leaks involve more than one party, such as an owner, a tenant and a property manager.

Older homes here were originally farmhouses on large agricultural plots. Newer multifamily housing is concentrated along Geneva Road and west of it. The plan is inconsistent about build years, so this page does not state an era. Different houses use different materials, and a restoration crew reads each one on site.

The plan maps stormwater detention basins and reports no flooding incidents. Neighborhood-specific FEMA flood-zone status not verified. Check the FEMA Flood Map Service Center for your exact address, because a zone applies to mapped areas and not to a whole neighborhood.

Common leak sources in houses and shared buildings

Dishwasher and washer lines

A split supply hose or a stuck drain can soak a kitchen or laundry floor and the unit below it.

Water heaters

A tank leak or a failed relief line can spread to nearby walls and carpet. See water heater leak cleanup.

Upstairs units

A bathroom or kitchen leak above can show up as a stained or sagging ceiling below.

Supply line failures

A burst line behind a wall can wet several rooms. See burst pipe water damage cleanup.

How drying works across rooms and units

  1. Stop the source

    Someone shuts off the fixture valve or the main, if it is safe and reachable.

  2. Extract and map

    Standing water comes out first. Meters then check drywall, flooring and framing, including walls shared with a neighbor.

  3. Set drying equipment

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run in each affected space. Structural drying and dehumidification explains the process.

  4. Recheck readings

    Readings repeat until materials return to normal. ANSI/IICRC S500 is the published reference standard. For a broader overview, see water damage cleanup.

Shared buildings and who does what

In a townhome, condo or apartment, the shut-off may be in a utility closet, a shared riser or a unit you cannot open. Ask your property manager or association where it is before a leak happens.

Roles differ by building. Check your lease or governing documents rather than assuming, and tell the property manager in writing as soon as you find water. Drying may need to reach more than one unit, so the person you speak with will ask who can grant access.

Safety steps while the water is still there

Keep people and pets out of contaminated or electrically hazardous water. Do not use electrical equipment or touch breakers while standing in water. Shut off water only if you can reach the valve safely.

Call 911 or the utility for sparks, a gas smell, or sewage at outlets. Sewage and storm water are Category 3, so do not dry them yourself and do not use ordinary shop vacs or household fans. Photograph damage when safe and lift unaffected items only when safe.

Insurance and documentation

Coverage depends on the cause and policy terms, so ask your insurer. Renters and owners often carry different policies. Photos, moisture readings and an itemized scope help document the loss.

A 2023 Herald Extra article reported that most homeowners insurance does not cover flooding and that flood insurance takes 30 days to take effect. Confirm which rules apply to you.

What to tell the person on the phone

  • Whether you own, rent or manage the unit
  • Where the leak started and what failed
  • Whether water reached a unit above or below you
  • Where the shut-off is and whether it is off
  • Whether anyone can give access to neighboring units
