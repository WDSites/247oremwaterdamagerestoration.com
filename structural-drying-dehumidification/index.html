Structural Drying and Dehumidification in Orem, UT

Structural drying pulls trapped moisture out of the framing, floors and walls of your Orem home, and it is measured, not guessed.

☎ Call (385) 988-9410

What structural drying is

Structural drying and dehumidification in Orem, UT is the controlled removal of moisture from building materials after a water loss, using air movers, dehumidifiers and daily meter readings until the materials reach their dry standard. It happens after the standing water is gone and the source is stopped.

The first 72 hours of a loss, covered on the water mitigation page, are about stopping the damage. This page covers the method itself, which is the part that takes the most days and the most equipment.

How water gets trapped in Orem buildings

Orem was incorporated in 1919 largely to build a water system, according to the Utah State History Encyclopedia, and homes here have been tied to that system ever since. Today the water that wets a house is usually a supply line, a leaking appliance, a roof leak or runoff, and it soaks into drywall, insulation, subfloors and framing within hours.

Frame construction holds water in places you cannot see, such as bottom plates, wall cavities and the layer under flooring. A floor can look dry on top while the subfloor stays wet. Ask the crew to show you readings from several spots, not just the surface.

When drying should start

Call quickly once the water has stopped, because drywall paper, wood subfloors and insulation hold moisture and begin to degrade the longer they stay wet. If water is still spreading, the first call is for shutoff and extraction, and drying follows.

Our dispatch line is open around the clock and same-day response is offered when available. You do not need to wait for the carpet to feel soaked. A damp baseboard or a cool, clammy wall is reason enough to have it checked with a moisture meter.

Safety steps before drying equipment goes in

  • Make sure the water source is stopped at the fixture or main.
  • Keep cords off wet floors and plug equipment into dry, grounded outlets only.
  • If outlets or the panel were wet, call 911 or the utility before using any power.
  • If sewage was involved, stay out and treat it as a contamination job first.
  • Keep pets and children away from running equipment and open wall cavities.

Drying in plain terms

Drying works on three things the crew can control. Warm air holds more water vapor than cold air, moving air carries moisture off wet surfaces, and a dehumidifier pulls vapor out of that air so more can evaporate. Technicians track this as temperature, relative humidity and grains of moisture per pound of air.

Before the first machine runs, the crew takes a dry reference reading from unaffected material of the same type. That reading becomes the drying goal. The ANSI/IICRC S500 standard describes this process, and you can ask any company whether its crews work to it.

How a structural drying job runs

  1. Map the moisture

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera show where the water went, and the findings are marked on walls and floors.

  2. Set the drying goal

    The crew takes dry reference readings from unaffected materials so everyone knows what finished looks like.

  3. Remove what cannot dry

    Saturated pad, soaked insulation and swollen drywall come out so air can reach the framing.

  4. Place air movers

    Air movers are aimed along walls and floors, and some push air into wall cavities through small openings.

  5. Run LGR dehumidifiers

    Low grain refrigerant units pull vapor from the air, and desiccant units can help in cold, dry conditions.

  6. Monitor every day

    The crew returns to take readings, moves equipment and ends the job only when the materials match the goal.

Most homes in Orem need LGR units, since indoor air is warm enough for them to work well. Desiccants earn a place in winter when a space is cold and the moisture load is light.

Why drying takes days, not hours

Water inside wood, concrete and plaster moves out slowly, and dense materials hold it longest. A hardwood subfloor or a concrete slab can take several days even with the right equipment, while drywall and carpet dry faster.

We do not rent equipment, and a rented fan from a store will not reach the same result. Machines placed without readings often leave the framing damp, which is how mold and odor start. Drying is finished only when the meter says so.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not turn off the equipment because the room feels dry. Surface feel is a poor guide, and you can end up with swollen trim and a musty smell weeks later. Do not close vents or seal the room tight, since airflow and humidity control work together.

Do not start rebuilding too early. Orem's Building Safety Division asks for permits on many repairs and on plumbing and electrical work, so ask before closing walls back up. Rebuild belongs to the repair stage, not the drying stage.

Where drying fits in the larger job

Drying often overlaps with other steps. Wet carpet and pad, covered on the carpet water damage restoration page, may be pulled while the slab dries. Spaces under the floor, such as the ones on the crawl space cleanup page, need cold-weather equipment and extra caution.
