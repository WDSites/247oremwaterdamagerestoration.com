Emergency Frozen Pipe Water Damage Restoration in Orem, UT

Frozen pipes in Orem homes often split in the cold and only start flooding when the temperature climbs, so the damage shows up at thaw.

Frozen pipe damage, defined

If a pipe has frozen and you hear water or see a wet wall, call right away, because our dispatch line is open 24/7. Until a crew can get there, shut the main, open a faucet to drain the line and keep every heat source away from the wet area.

Frozen pipe water damage restoration in Orem, UT is the removal of water and the drying of a building after ice has split a pipe, fitting or hose bibb. It is the cold-weather cause. A sudden failure with no freezing is described on the burst pipe cleanup page, and the general first-72-hours work is covered under water mitigation.

How freezing pipes cause damage in Orem winters

Cold nights run from late fall into spring in Orem, and ice expands inside a pipe until the wall of the pipe gives way. The split often sits in a garage wall, a crawl space, an exterior wall or a hose bibb. Nothing leaks while the ice plugs the hole.

The flood starts at thaw. As the ice melts, water pours through the crack at full line pressure. This is a common Orem problem in cold snaps, though far fewer homes see it than see an ordinary supply-line leak. A house left empty over a holiday trip is the classic case, since nobody is home to hear it.

When a frozen pipe is an emergency

A pipe that is merely frozen, with no water coming out of the tap and no wet spots, can usually be thawed carefully. A pipe that is frozen and splitting is different. You may see a stain, hear dripping inside a wall, or notice the faucet works but the pressure is low.

When the break thaws, the home can flood in minutes. Call the dispatch line at night or on a weekend, because the crews we send offer same-day response when available. Then close the main, because that stops the water that will come when the ice melts.

Thawing safety steps

  • Never use an open flame, torch or kerosene heater to thaw a pipe.
  • Use a hair dryer or warm towels, and keep the dryer away from standing water.
  • Keep power off to wet rooms if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • If you smell gas or see sparks, leave and call 911 or the gas utility before calling us.
  • Open the faucet so melting ice and steam have a place to go.
  • If the pipe is inside a wall, leave it alone and call a plumber and the dispatch line.

How the crew restores a house after a frozen pipe

  1. Confirm the water is off

    The crew checks the shutoff first. A frozen pipe that has not fully thawed can reopen after the shutoff, so they look for the break rather than trusting the valve.

  2. Locate hidden breaks

    A thermal imaging camera and a moisture meter trace wet framing, insulation and subfloor inside walls and crawl spaces where the split is not visible.

  3. Pump and extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull thaw water off floors and out of carpet. Crawl spaces and garage slabs are drained before drying begins.

  4. Open the wet cavities

    Wet insulation and soaked drywall at the base of a wall are removed. Small access holes let air reach cavities that can still be saved.

  5. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers run for days. Readings are checked daily against dry reference points, following the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard as the published reference.

  6. Treat and document

    Antimicrobial treatment goes on where water sat, and photos and moisture logs go in the file for your insurer.

Ice, thaw timing and hidden breaks

Ice acts like a cork. The pipe can hold shut for hours or days, and you may not find the split until the afternoon sun hits the wall or the furnace warms the garage. That is why a house with no visible leak at 6 a.m. can have a flooded crawl space by evening.

Walk the home after a deep freeze. Look at the exterior wall plumbing under sinks, the water heater closet and the crawl space entrance. A damp baseboard, a moldy smell or a stained ceiling after the cold breaks is worth a call. Water heater leaks have their own page, the water heater leak cleanup page.

Secondary water, hose bibbs and winter prevention in Lindon and Orem

Lindon City Public Works says the secondary irrigation system is not drinking water, usually runs from about late April to mid-October, and that residents must drain sprinkler lines and shut their secondary valves to prevent winter damage and spring flooding. Do that before the first hard freeze. That fits our work in Lindon and in nearby Pleasant Grove.

Disconnect garden hoses, insulate pipes in garage and crawl space walls, and let a faucet drip on the coldest nights. Know the city meter location too, since Lindon's public works says the owner is responsible for the line past the meter. Rentals near Utah Valley University have another layer: tell the landlord in writing and keep photos.

Materials involved

Insulation, drywall, flooring and cabinets each respond differently to wetting.

Mistakes to avoid with frozen pipes

Raising the thermostat only after the pipe freezes is too late. Run warm air into the area instead, and leave cabinet doors open so heat reaches pipes under sinks. Skipping the main shutoff after a thaw is the costliest mistake, because the split opens when you are not watching.

Do not ignore a frozen line with no sign of damage. It can still hold a crack. Have a plumber check it before the next cold snap.
