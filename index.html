What water damage restoration involves in Orem

Water damage restoration is the work of stopping a water source, removing standing water, drying wet building materials, cleaning and sanitizing the affected rooms, and rebuilding what could not be saved. If you are dealing with water damage in Orem, UT, the first day matters most, because wet drywall, carpet pad and subfloor begin to break down fast. 247 Orem Water Damage Restoration is a 24/7 dispatch for licensed and insured restoration professionals serving Orem and the towns around it.

Most jobs move through four stages. Mitigation stops the loss from growing. Cleanup pulls out debris and contaminated materials. Structural drying brings wood, concrete and drywall back to a safe moisture level. Repair then replaces drywall, flooring, trim and paint. Each stage has its own page on this site, and the sections below show how they fit together.

The water that reaches Orem buildings comes from a short list of sources. Supply lines fail, water heaters rust through, sinks overflow, roofs leak, and winter freezes split pipes. The response is similar every time. Find the wet material before it spreads, pull the water out, and dry the structure with equipment you can measure instead of guessing.

How a water damage job runs from the first call

  1. Call and triage

    You reach the 24/7 line and the dispatcher asks where the water is coming from, how far it has spread, and whether anyone has seen sewage, sagging ceilings or a gas smell. Electrical and gas risks go to 911 or the utility first. Then the dispatcher sends licensed and insured restoration professionals and tells you what to do while you wait.

  2. Stop the source

    The crew shuts the fixture valve, the water heater supply or the main valve. If the break is on the city side of the meter, you call the city water line instead, because the crew cannot work on the utility's pipe.

  3. Extract the standing water

    Submersible pumps handle deep water. Truck-mounted and portable extraction units pull water out of carpet, pad and hard floors. Every gallon removed this way is a gallon the air movers do not have to evaporate.

  4. Map the moisture

    Moisture meters read drywall, framing and subfloor. Thermal imaging cameras show cold, wet patches behind finished walls and under flooring. The crew marks the wet boundary and takes baseline readings to compare against later.

  5. Dry the structure

    Air movers push dry air across wet surfaces while LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers pull moisture out of the air. Crews return to check readings. Drying usually takes days, not hours, and depends on the materials involved.

  6. Clean, sanitize and hand off to repair

    Surfaces get cleaned and treated with antimicrobial products. HEPA air scrubbers run where dust or contamination was disturbed. When readings reach the dry standard, the job moves to rebuild work.

Why Orem buildings take on water

Orem was incorporated in 1919, largely to build a municipal water system, and the city still runs its own culinary water through the Water Section of Public Works. About 23,530 connections draw from nine deep wells, two mountain springs and treated Provo River water. The system is old civic infrastructure, so ask about the age of your service line and learn where your main shutoff and the city meter sit before something breaks.

The Provo River is the main flood concern. Runoff from the Dry Canyon drainage northeast of Orem and Lindon is directed to a detention basin near the Dry Canyon trailhead. In the high-runoff spring of 2023, Orem Public Works kept nearly 5,000 sandbags available and served residents next to the river first. Clear gutters and storm drains, aim downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the foundation, and sign up for alerts at alerts.utahcounty.gov.

Freezing nights from late fall into spring split pipes in unheated garages, crawl spaces and hose bibs. Near Utah Valley University and along State Street (US 89), many buildings are rentals, where one failed supply line can wet several rooms. If you rent, tell your landlord the moment you see water and photograph everything before anything is moved.

Summer brings its own failures. AC condensate lines clog and back up into closets and ceilings, washer valves let go, and sump pumps quit during the heaviest runoff. Window wells fill and push water along basement walls. Replace rubber washer hoses with braided ones, flush condensate lines at the start of the cooling season, and test the sump pump before the spring melt.

Water categories and what the crew does about each

Restoration professionals sort water by contamination level, a system used in the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard for water damage restoration. The category decides what can be dried in place and what must be removed.

CategoryWhat it isWhat the crew does
Category 1Clean water from a supply line, tub overflow or broken water heaterExtracts, dries in place and saves most materials if acted on quickly
Category 2Gray water from a washer, dishwasher or sump overflowExtracts, cleans, removes porous materials like carpet pad, treats with antimicrobial
Category 3Sewage, river or storm flood waterRemoves porous materials, cuts out affected drywall, disinfects, dries and clears with air scrubbers

Clean water does not stay clean forever. Category 1 water left sitting for a couple of days can pick up bacteria from the materials it soaks, which is one reason the first day matters so much.

What drives the cost of a water damage job

No honest number can be given without seeing the room. These are the factors that move a job from small to large.

Area and volume affected

A single wet bathroom wall and a flooded finished basement are different jobs. More wet square footage means more extraction, more air movers and more drying days.

Water category

Sewage and flood water call for removal and disinfecting instead of drying in place, which adds labor and disposal.

Materials that got wet

Hardwood, plaster, insulation and carpet pad react very differently. Some can be dried, others have to be cut out and replaced.

Time before drying starts

Water that sits for days spreads into framing and sub-flooring. Early response usually keeps the scope smaller.

Access and equipment days

Crawl spaces, finished ceilings and crowded rooms make drying harder. Equipment stays on site until readings come back dry, so the count of days affects the total.

Insurance depends on how the damage happened. Sudden and accidental water damage, like a burst pipe, is often covered, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded from standard homeowners policies and needs separate flood coverage, so check your policy or ask your insurer.

Mold is a secondary service, and it starts with moisture

Mold needs moisture, so the first question after any water event is whether everything dried completely. If a wall, carpet or ceiling stayed damp, mold can start to grow there. Crews that handle it work to the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation, using containment barriers with negative air so spores are not spread to clean rooms.

Health questions about mold belong to a physician, not a restoration crew. Whether Utah licenses mold work is worth asking, along with what the company can show you. When the problem is already visible, the mold remediation page explains the process.

Documenting a large loss for your insurer

Take wide and close photos of every wet room before furniture is moved. Keep damaged items until your adjuster has seen them or approved disposal. Save receipts for hotel stays, temporary repairs and replacement items.

Ask the crew for the moisture readings, equipment logs and a written scope of work. Those records show what was wet and what was done about it. Your insurer decides what is covered, so ask them directly.
