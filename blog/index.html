What you will find in these guides

These articles answer the questions Orem homeowners ask when water shows up where it should not be. Each guide explains the steps, the tools a crew uses, the common mistakes, and when a phone call is the better move than a weekend project.

Topics include how restoration works from start to finish, how hardwood floors and framing lumber behave when wet, how to deal with mold after a leak, and how to think about an insurance claim.

How the guides are written

Every article is written for homeowners, not for trade insiders. We use plain words, name the equipment such as moisture meters, thermal imaging cameras, air movers and LGR dehumidifiers, and explain why each step happens.

We do not print prices in the guides. Cost depends on the water, the materials and the time involved, so the articles describe the factors and leave the number to a crew that has seen your property.

What these guides are not

The articles are general information. They are not legal, medical, engineering or insurance advice, and they cannot replace a walkthrough by a licensed and insured professional. Health questions about mold belong with a physician, and coverage questions belong with your insurer.

When to stop reading and call

If water is still spreading, if it has touched an electrical panel or sewage, or if a wall or ceiling is sagging, skip the reading. Call 911 or the utility for electrical or gas risk, then call the 24/7 line. You can come back to the guides once the situation is under control.

Need help with a specific service

If you already know what you need, go straight to water mitigation, water damage repair or mold remediation. If you are unsure, the services overview has a table that matches symptoms to services, and the service area page lists the towns we dispatch to.
