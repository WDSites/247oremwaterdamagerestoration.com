Basement Mold Control in Orem, UT

Mold needs moisture, so a dry basement is the real prevention plan. Here is how to build one.

By the 247 Orem Water Damage Restoration team

Control moisture and you control mold

Mold spores are in ordinary air, so you cannot keep them out of a house. What you can control is whether they find a damp surface to settle on. A basement stays clear of mold when its air, walls, and floor stay dry, and every tip in this guide aims at that goal.

Basements are the hardest room in a house to keep dry. They sit against soil, hold the laundry and the water heater, and often get less airflow than upstairs rooms. A few cheap habits and one or two good machines do most of the work.

Start with a humidity reading

A digital hygrometer costs little and tells you more than your nose does. Place one in the middle of the basement and another in the dampest corner. Many building guides suggest keeping indoor relative humidity below about 50 percent, and basements often need a machine to hold that line in summer.

Check readings at different times of day for a week. A spike after laundry or showers points to household moisture. A steady high number even with the washer off points to moisture coming through the walls or slab.

Dehumidifiers that actually help

Size a dehumidifier by the square footage and dampness of the space, and check the rating on the box. A unit that is too small runs all day without reaching your target. Place it in the open, away from walls, and keep the door to the room closed.

Use a hose to drain it to a floor drain or sump pit so the bucket never fills and shuts the machine off. Clean the filter on schedule and empty any bucket daily. A dehumidifier is a tool for managing humidity, not a repair for a leak, and it will not dry a flooded room.

Window wells, gutters, and grading

  1. Clear the window wells

    Remove leaves, dirt, and trash from each well in fall and again in spring. Check that gravel at the bottom still drains. A clear cover that fits snugly keeps rain and snow out.

  2. Clean gutters and extend downspouts

    Overflowing gutters dump water along the foundation. Add downspout extensions that carry water several feet from the wall, and check that none ends in a low spot.

  3. Check the slope

    Soil beside the house should fall away from the foundation, not toward it. Add fill soil where it has settled, and keep flower beds and mulch from blocking drainage.

  4. Aim irrigation away

    Sprinkler heads that spray the wall soak the foundation every watering cycle. Adjust them and watch for wet patches the day after.

  5. Test the sump pump

    Pour a bucket of water into the pit and watch the float lift and the pump discharge. Confirm the discharge line carries water away from the house and does not freeze in winter.

Indoor habits that add up

  • Run bath fans and the dryer vent to the outside, never into the basement
  • Fix drips under sinks and at washer hoses as soon as you see them
  • Keep stored items a few inches off the floor and away from exterior walls
  • Dry wet towels, rugs, and sports gear upstairs instead of hanging them downstairs
  • Leave a gap behind furniture so air can move along cold walls
  • Replace water-stained ceiling tiles and drywall instead of painting over them

A seasonal routine that fits a basement

In spring, test the sump pump, clear the window wells, and look along the foundation for new damp lines. In summer, watch the hygrometer closely, because warm air carries more moisture and condenses on cool concrete. Keep the dehumidifier running on a drain hose.

In fall, pull hoses off outdoor spigots, clean gutters after the leaves drop, and reset the downspout extensions. In winter, check that sump discharge lines have not frozen and that no pipe on an exterior wall has started to drip. A ten minute walk through the basement each season catches most problems early.

When prevention is not enough

If you can see staining that spreads, smell a persistent musty odor, or find damp drywall, treat it as a moisture problem first. Fix the source, dry the materials, and then deal with the growth. Cleaning mold without drying the surface only gives it something to return to.

Larger growth, mold inside walls, or any growth after flooding calls for containment and a work plan that follows the ANSI/IICRC S520 standard for mold remediation. Health questions belong to a physician, and nothing in this guide diagnoses anyone.

When to call a professional

Call when a basement has taken on water, when drywall or carpet stays damp for more than a day, or when stains keep spreading after you fix the leak. Early water mitigation removes the moisture that growth depends on, and mold remediation handles contained removal if growth has already formed.

What Orem homeowners should know

The main flood concern in Orem is the Provo River, and the Dry Canyon drainage northeast of Orem and Lindon drains to a detention basin near the Dry Canyon trailhead. During the high-runoff spring of 2023, Orem Public Works kept nearly 5,000 sandbags available, starting with residents next to the Provo River. If your lot sits near either waterway, check your window wells and sump pit before the snowmelt starts.

The city promotes alerts at alerts.utahcounty.gov and asks residents to clear gutters, curbs, and storm drains and to point downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the house. Those are the same moves that protect a basement from mold. Sign up for the alerts and walk your property after the first warm week of spring.
