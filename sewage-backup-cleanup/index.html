Emergency Sewage Backup Cleanup in Orem, UT

Sewage coming up through a drain in your Orem home is a contamination emergency, and the first job is keeping people out of it.

What sewage backup cleanup means

Sewage backup cleanup in Orem, UT is the removal of wastewater that has come up through a floor drain, toilet, tub or basement fixture, followed by sanitizing everything it touched. This is an emergency, and our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait, keep people and pets out of the room, close the door, and do not run any more water down the drains.

Wastewater is classed as Category 3, the most contaminated category in the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard. That label changes the work. Crews wear protective gear, bag and remove porous materials, and treat the space as contaminated until it has been cleaned and dried.

Where the water comes from in Orem homes

Orem's sewer flows to the Orem Water Reclamation Facility, according to the city's 2022 Water Conservation Plan. A backup can start in your own house line or farther down in the city main, and the two have different owners. If several neighbors are seeing the same thing, or the toilet upstairs bubbles when you flush downstairs, call Orem Public Works about the main while a plumber checks your line.

Basements are where most backups surface, because floor drains and basement bathrooms sit lowest on the line. Sewage backups are far less common here than burst pipes or appliance leaks, but when one happens it is usually a clogged lateral from roots, grease or wipes that never should have gone down the toilet.

When sewage in the house is an emergency

Treat any sewage on the floor as urgent, even a small amount. The water keeps spreading into carpet, drywall and cabinet bases, and the longer it sits, the more material has to be thrown out. Our after-hours dispatch handles these calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, with same-day response when available.

The dispatcher will ask where the water entered, how far it has spread and whether it reached electrical outlets or a furnace. Have those answers ready, plus a few photos taken from the doorway before anything is moved.

First-hour safety steps

  • Stay out of the water and keep children and pets away from it.
  • Cut power to the area only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • If water touches outlets, a furnace or a panel, or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before calling us.
  • Do not use a household vacuum on sewage. It can spread contamination through the exhaust.
  • Wash hands thoroughly after any contact and ask a physician about exposure questions.

How the crew cleans up sewage

  1. Assess and mark the affected area

    The crew checks where the sewage traveled with moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera, and sets the boundary of what is contaminated.

  2. Suit up and contain

    Protective gear goes on, and plastic sheeting and tape can seal doorways so contamination stays out of clean rooms.

  3. Extract the liquid

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing wastewater out, and the first pass removes the bulk before anything is touched.

  4. Remove what cannot be cleaned

    Carpet, pad, wet drywall, insulation and particleboard that absorbed sewage are cut out, bagged and carried off.

  5. Clean and sanitize

    Remaining surfaces are scrubbed, then treated with an antimicrobial product, and HEPA air scrubbers can run to clean the air.

  6. Dry and verify

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers dry framing and concrete, and readings are compared against a dry reference before the space is released.

Why sewage cleanup is different from clear-water cleanup

A burst supply line sends clean water that can often be dried in place. Sewage does the opposite, because the water itself is the contaminant. Standard water damage cleanup steps still apply, but removal comes earlier and drying comes later.

If the backup came from a basement drain after a storm, groundwater and sewer water may have mixed, and the job leans toward flood damage restoration. Ask your crew which category applies, since it decides what leaves the house.

What shapes the cost of sewage cleanup

Area and depth

More square footage and deeper water mean more extraction and more material removed.

Materials involved

Carpet and drywall come out and are replaced later, while concrete and framing are cleaned in place.

Time before cleanup begins

Sewage that has soaked in for days reaches higher up the walls and into more layers.

Access and containment

Finished basements with tight stairs or shared air paths need more setup before work starts.

Drying needs

Wet framing and slab can take several days to dry, and equipment time adds to the total.

Insurance treats sewer and drain backup differently from other water losses. Many standard policies exclude it unless an endorsement was added, and flood damage is usually excluded entirely and needs separate flood coverage, as the Utah Insurance Department's flood FAQ explains. Check your policy or ask your insurer before you assume either way.

Mistakes to avoid

Do not try to mop and bleach a sewage backup yourself. Bleach does not reach contamination inside carpet pad or drywall, and fumes in a closed basement add their own problem. Do not hold wet items for later, since sorting waits while mold and odor build.

Do not skip the permit question on rebuild work. Orem's Building Safety Division requires permits for plumbing and electrical work and for many repairs, so ask before cutting new drywall back in. If the source turns out to be a failed lateral, a licensed plumber will handle that repair.

After the cleanup

Once the area is sanitized and dry, rebuild questions begin. If dampness lingers, a follow-up on mold remediation may be needed, since mold grows wherever moisture stays. Crews also reach neighbors in Vineyard and Pleasant Grove for the same kind of call, and the steps are the same there.
