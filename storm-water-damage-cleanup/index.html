When storm water is an emergency

Storm water damage is the cleanup and drying of a building after rain, snowmelt or runoff enters through openings at or below grade, and it is a daily concern across Orem, UT during heavy weather. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, so call once water crosses a threshold or rises in a window well. While you wait, lift valuables to higher shelves and keep everyone out of rooms with wet outlets.

This page covers water that arrives from outside in modest volume. A swollen river or a whole street under water belongs on the flood damage restoration page, and once the water is simply sitting in a room, emergency water removal describes the pumping side.

How storm water finds Orem homes

Snowmelt in spring and sudden storms are the usual triggers, and the entry points repeat from house to house. Window wells fill when the drain at the bottom clogs, garage doors let water slide under the seal, and a flat or reversed grade steers runoff toward the foundation instead of away from it. Storm drains choke with leaves and gravel, and the water finds the lowest door.

Orem Public Works told residents in the high-runoff spring of 2023 to clear gutters, curbs and storm drains and to point downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the house. That is cheap work to do before a storm. Walk the lot once, check that each downspout empties several feet from the wall, and confirm the sump discharge line is not buried or frozen.

Honest odds: how often storm water is the problem here

Most Orem water losses start inside the house, from supply lines, water heaters and appliances. Storm intrusion is less common, but it clusters in a few weeks of snowmelt and the occasional hard cloudburst, then arrives at many homes at once. That is why calls bunch up, and why same-day response when available depends on how many properties are affected.

Real cases look ordinary. A window well that filled and leaked through a basement window, a garage floor with an inch of runoff, a finished room where the sump pump quit during a long soaking. Few involve a creek leaving its banks, which is the flood page.

Storm water safety steps before anything else

Water that touches outlets, extension cords or the electrical panel is a shock risk. Do not wade through it. Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach, and if you see sparks or smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before you call us. Runoff in a garage or basement can also carry fuel, fertilizer and sewage, so keep children and pets out.

Photograph the water line on walls and the items affected before you move anything. The pictures help later with your insurer, and a quick note of the time the water started adds useful detail.

What the crew does

  1. Confirm the entry point

    The crew finds where the water is getting in, such as a well, door sill or foundation crack, and notes whether it is still coming.

  2. Pump and extract

    Submersible pumps move deep water, and truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull the rest from carpet, pad and hard floors.

  3. Check what the water touched

    Surface runoff is usually treated as Category 2 or worse because it crosses soil and pavement, so porous materials such as pad and lower drywall often come out.

  4. Map the moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far the water wicked into walls, plates and subfloor.

  5. Set up drying

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on the affected rooms, following the drying approach in ANSI/IICRC S500, the published water damage restoration standard.

  6. Clean, treat and recheck

    Surfaces get cleaned, antimicrobial treatment goes on, and readings are repeated daily until the materials match a dry reference.

Sandbags, alerts and what to watch

In spring 2023, Orem Public Works kept nearly 5,000 sandbags available and began with residents next to the Provo River. Utah County also promotes alerts at alerts.utahcounty.gov, which is worth bookmarking before runoff season. Sign up, then stack bags at the specific door or well that has failed before, not across the whole yard. Homeowners in northeast Orem can see how the city's 2024 storm reporting applies in our Cascade neighborhood guide.

Nearby towns have lived the same pattern. In May 2005, Grove Creek runoff in Pleasant Grove backed up at a canal and drew about 450 volunteers to fill sandbags, and in 2023 American Fork crews cleared storm grates and filled over 5,000 sandbags. Check your own storm grate and curb the same way.

Roof and ceiling water from the same storm

Wind-driven rain can also come in from above. If a ceiling is stained or sagging after the same storm, read about roof leak water damage cleanup. We dry and clean the interior only, so call a roofer for the shingles or flashing.

What shapes the cost of storm water cleanup

Depth and duration

A few inches that sat for an hour need less pumping and drying than a basement that stood in water overnight.

Water category

Runoff and drain water are treated as contaminated, which means more removal and cleaning than clean-water jobs.

Materials affected

Carpet pad, drywall and insulation come out more often than tile or concrete, and each adds labor and disposal.

Square footage and floors

More wet area means more air movers and dehumidifiers running for more days.

Access and hours

Finished basements with tight stairs and after-hours calls change how the crew staffs the job.

Insurance depends on how the water arrived. A policy may treat sudden storm damage differently from seepage or neglect, and the Utah Insurance Department's flood FAQ notes that standard homeowners policies do not cover flood, while NFIP policies usually carry a 30 day waiting period. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and keep the photographs.

Mistakes to avoid after a storm

Do not run a household vacuum on a wet floor, and do not trust a fan alone to dry a wall. Do not paint or re-carpet until readings are dry, because trapped moisture shows up as stains and odor weeks later.

Skip the urge to stack sandbags against a door that opens inward, since water already inside has nowhere to go. Build the barrier outside. Ask Orem's Building Safety Division whether any rebuild work needs a permit before walls are opened.
