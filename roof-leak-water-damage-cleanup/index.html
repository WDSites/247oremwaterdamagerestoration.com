What roof leak water damage cleanup covers

If water is coming through a ceiling right now, this is an emergency, and our dispatch line is open 24/7. While you wait, set out buckets, move furniture and electronics from under the drip, and keep people away from any ceiling that bulges.

Roof leak water damage cleanup in Orem, UT is the removal of water and the drying of the attic, insulation, ceiling and wall cavities after water enters from above. We do not repair or tarp roofs. Call a roofer for the roof itself, and call us for the wet interior. Rebuilding finished surfaces afterward is covered under water damage repair.

How roof leaks happen in Orem homes

Snow sits on Orem roofs through the winter, and a warm attic can melt the bottom layer so the water refreezes at the eave in a ridge of ice. Water backed up behind that ridge finds nail holes and seams. The same thing happens when spring melt arrives faster than gutters can drain.

Orem Public Works asks residents to keep gutters, curbs and storm drains clear and to point downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the house. That advice protects the foundation, and it also keeps roof water from backing up at the eave. Clean your gutters before the first snow and again in spring.

When a roof leak is an emergency

A small stain that has not grown is a call for the next business day. A ceiling that is dripping, bulging or darkening is not. Water pooled above drywall adds weight, and a ceiling holding that weight can let go at any time.

We take calls at night, on weekends and on holidays, and the crews we dispatch offer same-day response when available. Tell the dispatcher whether the ceiling is sagging and whether it is near a light fixture, so the crew brings the right extraction units and containment supplies.

Ceiling collapse safety steps

  • Stay out of any room where the ceiling is bulging, cracking or dripping steadily.
  • Turn off power to that room only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • If water reaches a light fixture, outlet or panel, or you smell gas, leave and call 911 or the utility before calling us.
  • Put a bucket under steady drips and a tarp or plastic over furniture you cannot move.
  • Do not climb onto a wet roof to look for the leak.
  • Photograph the ceiling, the attic and any damaged contents before cleanup starts.

How the crew dries a roof leak loss

  1. Find where the water went

    A moisture meter and a thermal imaging camera map wet drywall, insulation and framing. The attic is checked as well, because the worst wetness is often above the visible stain.

  2. Relieve and remove

    A sagging ceiling is opened in a controlled way so trapped water drains. Soaked insulation and ceiling drywall that cannot dry are bagged and removed.

  3. Extract what reached the floor

    Portable extraction units pull water off carpet and hard floors under the leak.

  4. Dry the cavity

    Air movers and LGR (low grain refrigerant) dehumidifiers run until the readings match a dry reference. Details of the method are on the structural drying page.

  5. Clean and treat

    Antimicrobial treatment goes on where water sat, and surfaces are cleaned. Contents are sorted into what can be dried and what should be discarded.

  6. Document for the roofer and insurer

    Photos and moisture readings support the claim and help the roofer see where water entered. Work follows the ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage restoration standard as the published reference.

Snowmelt, ice dams and other storm-related entry points

Not every roof leak is a hole. Ice dams push meltwater under shingles, gutters overflow into the soffit, and wind-driven rain forces water past flashing. These leaks are often slow, so a ceiling may look dry for days before a stain appears.

If the water entered through a window well, garage or grade instead of the roof, that is covered on the storm water damage cleanup page. Roof leaks are less common in Orem than plumbing leaks, but they cluster after heavy snow years. If the roof is old, ask a roofer to inspect it before the next storm.
