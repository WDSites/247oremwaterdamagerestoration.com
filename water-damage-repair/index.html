What water damage repair covers

Water damage repair is the rebuilding work that follows drying, and it brings walls, ceilings, floors and cabinets in Orem, UT back to a finished state. Drywall gets cut out and replaced, insulation is renewed, flooring is relaid or refinished, and the surfaces are primed, textured and painted to match. This is the stage people usually picture first, but it only works on materials that have already been dried and verified.

If the water is still moving, start with water mitigation so the structure dries before it is closed up. Wet debris and contaminated materials are covered under water damage cleanup. Repair is not an emergency service, though our dispatch line takes calls any time you need to talk through what you are looking at.

Where repair calls come from

The most frequent repair stories begin with a small failure. A washer supply valve breaks and soaks the laundry room wall. A burst pipe under a sink wets the cabinet base. An upstairs toilet leaks into a finished basement ceiling, or an air conditioner condensate line backs up and stains a hallway ceiling. Roof leaks add brown rings and sagging drywall below.

Rebuilds in Orem can involve permits. The Orem Building Safety Division requires permits for work that remodels or repairs, with some exceptions, and for plumbing, electrical, heating or air-conditioning work. Ask Building Safety before you open walls, and ask any contractor who pulls the permit and who schedules the inspection.

Signs the damage needs more than a paint touch-up

  • A brown or yellow ring on a ceiling that keeps growing or returns after painting
  • Paint or wallpaper that bubbles, peels or flakes along a wall
  • Drywall that feels soft, crumbles at the corner or sags between joists
  • Baseboards that swell or pull away from the wall
  • Hardwood boards cupping at the edges or gaps opening between planks
  • Cabinet bases with swollen particleboard or a bowed kick plate
  • A ceiling that has partially collapsed or is hanging low

How repair work is assessed and carried out

  1. Confirm the materials are dry

    A moisture meter reads the drywall, studs and subfloor, and a thermal imaging camera checks for cold wet spots. Nothing gets closed in until readings match a dry reference spot in the same house.

  2. Mark the cut lines

    Damaged drywall is cut back to the nearest stud or joist, usually in a clean rectangle so the new patch can be fastened to framing.

  3. Replace insulation and treat framing

    Wet batt insulation is removed and replaced. Exposed framing is cleaned, dried and checked for soft spots before new board goes up.

  4. Hang, tape and finish

    New drywall is screwed to framing, taped with joint tape, coated with joint compound in thin passes and sanded smooth between coats.

  5. Match the texture

    Orange peel, knockdown or smooth finishes are sprayed or troweled to blend with the surrounding surface so the repair does not show in raking light.

  6. Prime and paint

    A stain-blocking primer seals any residual marks, then paint is rolled out to a natural break such as a corner or ceiling line.

  7. Reinstall flooring, trim and cabinets

    Flooring is relaid or refinished, baseboard and casing go back, and cabinet bases are replaced or reset.

Ceiling and wall water damage repair

Ceilings fail faster than walls because water pools on the back of the drywall and the paper face loosens. A bulging ceiling should be handled carefully, since it can hold a large amount of water. Poke a small hole in the lowest point over a bucket to release it, and wear eye protection. Do not stand directly beneath it.

Once the cavity is dry, the repair is straightforward. Drywall is cut out to joists, insulation is replaced, new board is hung and finished, and a stain-blocking primer goes on before paint. A collapsed ceiling is the same sequence with more demolition and disposal.

Wall repair follows the same logic, with extra attention to outlets and switches. If water reached an outlet box, a licensed electrician should check it before the wall is closed. Painting only the stained spot rarely works, so expect to paint the whole wall or ceiling plane to the nearest corner. Use a stain-blocking primer before the topcoat, because ordinary latex lets old rings bleed through.

Hardwood cupping and floor repair

Hardwood absorbs water through its underside, which makes the edges swell higher than the center. That is called cupping. If the boards are dried slowly and the cupping eases, a sanding and refinishing pass often fixes the surface. If the boards stay deformed or the finish is delaminating, planks or whole sections are replaced.

Engineered wood, laminate and vinyl plank behave differently. Laminate swells at the edges and rarely recovers. Vinyl plank may be dried and relaid if the subfloor is sound. A moisture meter on the subfloor tells you which path to take before any money is spent on new flooring.

Roof leak ceilings

A roof leak shows up as a brown ring far from the actual hole, because water follows rafters and ductwork before it drops. The first move is to fix the roof, since repairing the ceiling under an open leak means doing it twice. Roofing is outside what we do, so ask a roofer to repair the source.

After the roof is sealed, wet attic insulation is replaced, drywall below is cut out and replaced, and the ceiling is textured and painted. If the staining has a fuzzy or dark growth, stop and consider mold remediation before closing anything up.

The paint is bubbling but the drywall feels firm

Bubbling paint on firm drywall usually means water got into the paint film rather than the board, or the wall dried before the damage went deeper. Scrape the loose paint, let the wall sit, and read it with a moisture meter. If the reading matches a dry reference wall, sand, prime with a stain-blocking primer and repaint.

If the reading stays high, the cavity is still wet and a painted surface will fail again. That is a drying problem and belongs with mitigation. Repair is the last step, not the first, and rushing it is the most common mistake we hear about from callers.

What shapes the cost of water damage repair

Size of the damaged area

A patch behind a toilet differs from a ceiling that spans a room. Larger areas mean more board, tape, compound and paint.

Materials and finish level

Textured ceilings, crown molding, tile, hardwood and custom cabinets each add labor and matching time over plain painted drywall.

Hidden damage

Wet insulation, rotted framing, damaged wiring or mold found once walls are open can widen the scope.

Permits and inspections

Plumbing, electrical or structural work may need a permit, and inspections add scheduling steps.

Matching existing finishes

Discontinued flooring, older paint colors and unusual textures take extra effort to blend.
