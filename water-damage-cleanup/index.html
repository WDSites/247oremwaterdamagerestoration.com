The first 48 hours of water damage cleanup

If water has soaked your floors, the next two days decide how much of the house you keep, and our dispatch line runs 24/7 for homes across Orem, UT. Water damage cleanup is the removal of water, debris and contaminated materials, followed by sanitizing and deodorizing once the source is stopped. While you wait for a crew, stay out of any room where water touches outlets, lift valuables onto dry surfaces, and leave sagging ceilings alone.

This page covers the cleanup phase only. If a supply line is still running, the job starts with stopping the damage, and deep standing water needs pumps first through emergency water removal. Cleanup decides what leaves the building and what gets disinfected. Rebuilding the walls and floors afterward belongs to water damage repair.

Why cleanup jobs in Orem start from different places

Most losses trace back to a short list of sudden failures. Pipes freeze on cold nights from late fall into spring, water heaters split at the seam, washer supply valves let go, and air conditioner condensate lines clog in summer and spill into closets and ceilings. Snowmelt can also push water through a window well and into a finished basement, which changes the cleanup because ground water carries silt and bacteria.

Orem was incorporated in 1919 largely to build a water system, so the civic side of your plumbing is old infrastructure. The city serves about 23,530 water connections through the Water Section of Public Works. If a break sits on the street side of the meter, call the city water line. If it sits past the meter, it is yours, so find your main shutoff now, before the day you need it.

Signs you need cleanup and not just a mop

  • Carpet pad that squishes underfoot or smells sour a day after the leak
  • Drywall that is soft, stained at the base, or showing a wet line above the baseboard
  • Gray or brown water, or any sewage odor near a floor drain or toilet
  • Cabinet toe kicks that are swollen or lifting from the floor
  • Insulation visible through a ceiling hole and hanging heavy with water
  • A musty smell that returns after you air out the room

How a cleanup crew works through a wet house

  1. Walk the house and confirm the source is off

    The crew checks the shutoff, looks for live electrical hazards, and decides which rooms are safe to enter before any tools come out of the truck.

  2. Map the wet areas

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show where water traveled behind baseboards, under cabinets and up the wall, which is rarely where the stain is.

  3. Pull what cannot be saved

    Wet carpet pad, saturated insulation and soft drywall come out. Crews often make a flood cut about 12 inches above the waterline, then bag and carry the debris to the truck.

  4. Extract the remaining water

    Portable extraction units and truck-mounted equipment lift water from carpet, hard floors and low spots so the surfaces can start to dry.

  5. Wash and sanitize

    Hard surfaces get a HEPA vacuum pass, a detergent wash and an antimicrobial treatment. Grayer water means more aggressive washing and more removal.

  6. Deodorize the air and the materials

    HEPA air scrubbers pull fine particles out of the room, and odor-control treatment is applied to surfaces that held the smell.

  7. Pack out or protect belongings

    Dry contents are moved or blocked up off the floor. Wet items are sorted into clean, restorable and discard piles.

  8. Document the finished condition

    The crew records final moisture readings and photos, which helps when the rebuild or an insurer asks what was removed.

Belongings and contents

Wet belongings are where cleanup gets personal. Photo albums, paperwork, electronics and clothing each need a different move, and the wrong one makes things worse. Lift paper and fabric off wet floors immediately, and set electronics aside unplugged and untouched until someone checks them.

Take photos of everything before it is bagged or hauled. A room-by-room record of what was wet, what was cleaned and what was thrown out backs up any claim conversation with your insurer. It is far easier to build while the mess is still in front of you.

The floor looks dry but the room still smells

This is the most common cleanup gap. Surfaces dry quickly, but water that wicked into the base of a wall, under cabinet kicks or beneath laminate keeps feeding odor for days. A moisture meter and thermal camera find what the eye cannot, and the fix is usually removing a strip of baseboard or a section of drywall and treating the cavity.

If the smell comes with visible fuzzy growth or dark spots, the situation has moved into mold remediation, which uses containment and different rules than ordinary cleanup. Do not spray scented products over it. Masking the odor leaves the damp material in place.

What to do before the cleanup crew arrives

Cleanup goes faster and safer when the first hour is used well. These steps are the ones that matter most before anyone starts hauling out wet materials.

  • Stop the source. Shut the fixture valve or the main water valve, and keep the water off until the source is repaired.
  • Do not walk through wet areas near outlets. Turn power off at the panel only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without standing in water. Otherwise call 911 or the utility first.
  • Keep people and pets out of dirty water. Anything from a sewer line, toilet or outdoor flooding should be treated as contaminated until a crew says otherwise.
  • Lift what you can. Move small furniture, rugs, electronics and paperwork to a dry room. Leave heavy items for the crew.
  • Take photos before anything moves. Wide and close shots of the damage, plus the source, help later with the insurance conversation.

What shapes the cost of water damage cleanup

Water category

Clean supply water needs less removal than gray water from a washer or dishwasher, and sewage means far more material comes out and more protective gear goes on.

Square footage and rooms affected

A single bathroom is a different job than a main floor and a finished basement, because every extra room adds extraction, washing and equipment time.

Materials involved

Carpet over pad is quick to remove. Tile over a mortar bed, plaster or built-in cabinetry takes more labor and disposal.

How long water sat

A same-day response when available limits how far water travels, while a leak found days later usually means deeper cavity work and more odor treatment.

Contents handling

Packing out furniture, cleaning belongings off site and storing them adds labor that is separate from the structure itself.
