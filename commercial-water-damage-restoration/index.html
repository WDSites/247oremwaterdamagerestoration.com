What commercial water damage restoration covers

Commercial water damage restoration in Orem, UT is the extraction, drying and cleanup of water losses in offices, shops, small retail spaces, rentals and multi-unit buildings. Our dispatch line is open 24/7, and the crews we send are licensed and insured restoration professionals.

The work follows the same ANSI/IICRC S500 water damage standard used in homes, applied to larger floor plans, shared walls and spaces other people still need to use. This page explains who calls, what gets documented and how the work is staged.

Where commercial water loss happens in Orem

Orem's business activity runs along State Street (US 89) through the middle of the city, around University Place and Orem City Center, and near Utah Valley University and Timpanogos Regional Hospital. Rental units and mixed-use buildings fill much of that area, which means one burst line can reach several tenants.

Because ownership and occupancy are often split, ask early who holds the shutoff keys and who answers to the insurer. If you are on a lease, read the clause on water damage and notice. A short call to the landlord or property manager before the crew arrives saves time on the day.

Who calls and what each person decides

Building owner

Decides on repairs, owns the structure policy and approves scope when walls and ceilings are involved.

Property manager

Holds keys, shutoff locations and tenant contacts, and often coordinates access for the crew.

Tenant

Reports the loss, protects inventory and equipment, and tells the landlord right away.

Business operator

Decides what can be moved, what stays open and which rooms the crew can enter first.

When a business water loss is urgent

A running line, a sagging ceiling or standing water in a work area is urgent. Call quickly, since water travels between floors, under walls and into stock rooms, and the longer it sits, the more material has to come out.

While you wait, close the nearest shutoff, move inventory and electronics off the floor, and keep staff and customers out of wet areas. Same-day response is offered when available, and the dispatcher will explain next steps honestly instead of promising a time. For heavy standing water, the emergency water removal page explains pumping and extraction.

Safety steps for occupied buildings

  • Clear wet areas and block them off so staff and customers do not walk through.
  • Shut off water at the fixture, zone valve or main, whichever is closest.
  • Cut power only if the panel is dry and safe to reach.
  • If you see sparks, smell gas or find water at a panel, leave and call 911 or the utility first.
  • Keep sewage-contaminated areas closed and tell dispatch right away.

How the crew handles a commercial loss

  1. Confirm access and contacts

    Dispatch records who holds the keys, which units are affected and who approves work.

  2. Stop the source and extract

    Truck-mounted or portable extraction units pull standing water, and wet-dry vacs handle edges.

  3. Map the moisture

    Moisture meters and a thermal imaging camera show how far water traveled through ceilings and walls.

  4. Remove what cannot dry

    Ceiling tiles, wet drywall, soaked insulation and carpet tile may come out.

  5. Dry the structure

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers run on a schedule that fits the building, as described on the structural drying page.

  6. Clean and document

    Surfaces get antimicrobial treatment where needed, and photos and readings are kept for insurers and landlords.

Working around occupied spaces

Not every loss shuts a business. The crew can section off the wet area with plastic sheeting, route hoses and cords away from doorways, and run equipment at set hours when noise is an issue. Clear floor paths and tell the crew about tenants who stay on the premises.

Some tasks need a full closure of a room or suite, such as pulling a ceiling or lifting flooring. That call belongs to the owner or operator, and the dispatcher can explain which steps cannot be done around customers.

Documentation for insurers and landlords

Photograph the water, the source and every damaged item before anything moves. Keep the moisture readings and daily drying logs the crew produces, and save any written notices between tenant and landlord. A list of damaged stock or equipment is a useful extra.

Business interruption is an insurance question, not a promise from us. Check your policy or ask your insurer about it, and ask the property manager what the building's policy covers.

What shapes the cost of a commercial loss

Floor area affected

Larger wet areas need more extraction, more equipment and longer monitoring.

Building materials

Ceiling tile, carpet tile, drywall and concrete each dry differently.

Access and hours

Working around staff or after hours changes how the crew schedules the job.

Contamination

Clean water, gray water and sewage are handled differently, and removal adds work.

Drying time

Multi-unit and multi-floor losses often need more days of equipment and daily checks.

Sudden and accidental water damage is often covered under a commercial or landlord policy, while gradual leaks and neglect often are not. Flood damage is usually excluded and needs separate flood coverage. Check your policy or ask your insurer, and never assume approval.

Permits and what comes next

Once drying is done, rebuilding begins. Orem's Building Safety Division asks for permits on many repairs and on plumbing, electrical and mechanical work, so ask before opening or closing walls on a commercial rebuild.

Cleanup of debris and affected surfaces falls under water damage cleanup. Crews also serve nearby properties in Provo and Lindon, and the process is the same.
