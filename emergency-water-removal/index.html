Getting standing water out fast

Water on the floor is a clock running. Our line is open 24/7, so call as soon as you see it, then use the minutes before the crew arrives to cut the flow, protect what you can lift, and stay out of anything that touches power or sewage.

Emergency water removal in Orem, UT is the process of pumping and extracting standing water from a building with submersible pumps, truck-mounted extraction units and portable extractors. It is the first physical step in a water loss, ahead of drying, cleanup and repair.

When standing water is an emergency

A puddle that a towel can handle is not an emergency. Water deeper than the soles of your shoes, water in a finished basement, or water still arriving from a pipe or a window well is. Carpet absorbs and holds it, and the pad underneath acts like a sponge pressed against the subfloor.

Night or weekend does not change that, which is why the dispatch runs after hours and through holidays. We offer same-day response when available, and the dispatcher will give you an honest read on timing. If you must wait, shut off the water, move rugs and boxes onto tables or stairs, and leave the lights alone if the floor is wet.

Electricity and water together are the real danger. If water is near the panel, outlets or appliances, do not wade in. Turn off power at the breaker only if the panel is dry and you can reach it without stepping in water. For any shock risk, sparking or gas smell, call 911 or the utility first, then call us.

Where Orem water comes from

Orem basements tend to flood in one of four ways. A supply line or water heater fails, a sump pump quits during runoff, a window well fills and spills in, or a sewer line backs up through a floor drain. The first two are clean-water events and the last two can be contaminated, so tell the dispatcher which one you think it is.

The city says residents should clear gutters, curbs and storm drains and aim downspouts and sump pump discharge away from the house. In the high-runoff spring of 2023, Orem Public Works kept nearly 5,000 sandbags on hand and repeated that advice. Check your discharge hose and window well covers now, not during the next storm.

Next door, Lindon has a pressurized secondary irrigation system that is not drinking water and must be drained and shut off before winter. A forgotten valve is a common reason for spring flooding there. Our crews cross the line into Lindon regularly, and the same pumping applies.

Signs you need water removed now

  • Water visible across a floor, even a thin sheet Carpet and pad hold far more than they look like they do.
  • A basement floor drain that bubbles or burps
  • A sump pump that hums but does not move water The impeller may be jammed or the check valve stuck.
  • Water pushing under a door, along the baseboard or through a wall crack
  • A washer, dishwasher or water heater still leaking
  • Walls that feel damp to the touch above the water line Wicking carries water up drywall.
  • A smell like wet dirt or sewer

How the water comes out

  1. Make the scene safe

    The crew checks for electrical hazards, slick surfaces and contamination before setting a pump. Gloves, boots and respirators are used where water might carry sewage.

  2. Pump out the depth

    Submersible pumps clear anything deep enough to cover the intake. Discharge hoses run to a drain or outside, away from the foundation.

  3. Extract from surfaces

    Truck-mounted units, with strong vacuum and a long hose, pull water from carpet and pad. Portable extraction units reach upstairs or in tight rooms, and a wand tool pulls water from hard floors.

  4. Lift and remove what is ruined

    Soaked pad is cut and rolled out. Carpet is peeled back or taken out if the water was contaminated.

  5. Check what is left

    Moisture meters show what the extraction missed. Thermal imaging cameras help locate wet walls and subfloor.

  6. Hand off to drying

    Air movers and LGR dehumidifiers take over. See water mitigation for what that stage involves.

Burst pipe water damage

A burst pipe is one of the fastest ways to flood a house. A frozen line splits along its length, and the water often goes unnoticed until the thaw, when pressure returns and a full supply line empties into a wall or crawl space. Orem's freezing nights from late fall into spring make exterior walls, unheated garages and hose bibs the usual suspects.

Shut off the water at the main first. Then open the lowest faucet to drain remaining water from the line, and turn off the water heater if it feeds the broken section. Do not use a hair dryer or space heater near standing water.

Once the flow stops, the crew pumps out any pooled water, extracts the carpet and pad, and opens the wall below the break so water can drain and air can enter. A plumber must fix the pipe, since we remove the water and dry the structure but do not repair plumbing. Photograph the burst, the water line on the walls and any damaged belongings for your insurer.

Pumps, extractors or a wet vac

Each tool has a job, and using the wrong one wastes time. This is how crews choose between them.

Submersible pumpDeep water, basements, crawl spacesLeaves a film of water behind that must be extracted
Truck-mounted extraction unitLarge carpeted areas, long reachNeeds truck access to the property
Portable extractorUpstairs rooms, tight spaces, stairsSmaller capacity, more trips
Household wet vacSmall spills and cornersToo slow for anything that soaks pad

The household wet vac has its place, but a basement that took on an inch of water has hundreds of gallons in the carpet and pad. Renting or buying a small unit will not keep up, which is why people call for pumping.

Pumping out the basement without making it worse

If the water outside is high, do not drain a flooded basement all at once. Pumping too quickly can let wet soil press on the foundation walls. Let the crew advise on the pace. And never pump sewage into the yard or a storm drain.

What affects the price of water removal

Depth and volume of water

More gallons mean more pump time and more extraction passes.

Contamination level

Clean water is cheaper to handle than water that touched sewage or soil, which requires protective gear and disposal.

Access to the room

Stairs, narrow doors and long hose runs change how crews set up.

Floor type

Carpet and pad take more extraction effort than tile or sealed concrete.

Time of day and delay

Water that sat overnight has reached more materials than water caught within minutes.

After the water is out

Removal is the beginning. Left alone, damp framing and subfloor invite odor and mold within days, so drying equipment follows right behind the pumps. Once the building is dry, water damage cleanup handles sanitizing, and water damage repair rebuilds what was removed.

Restoration is not waterproofing. If your basement floods repeatedly, ask a waterproofing or foundation contractor about drainage. Also ask any restoration company whether its crews follow the ANSI/IICRC S500 standard and how they document the work.
